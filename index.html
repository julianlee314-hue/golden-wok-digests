The Golden Wok Digests

Old-school American Chinese buffet glory + simple mainland plates Western tables actually love

Pull up a chair. This book has two kitchens under one roof. Part I is the full-fat steam-table dream: General Tso’s, crab rangoon, crispy chow mein, combo fried rice — deep-fried when it should be, sweet-salty when it must be, built only for taste. Part II is old-school mainland comfort with the weird stuff left out: tomato-egg, scallion-oil noodles, hong shao rou, garlic greens — no organs, no bitter oddballs, no “interesting” textures that scare the table. Same page design as the Southern book: a little story, the recipe, and the secrets they don’t print on the takeout menu.

Home-kitchen adaptations of widely known Chinese and Chinese-American dishes. Not affiliated with any restaurant chain. Adjust chile heat to your table.

General Tso's Chicken

Kitchen note: Crispy dark-meat chicken in a glossy sweet-chile lacquer — buffet throne food.

Crispy dark-meat chicken in a glossy sweet-chile lacquer — buffet throne food. This version chases pure taste — crunchy, savory, a little sweet — no organs, no bitter oddities, no strange textures. Just what people actually order twice.

Serves 4

Ingredients

  • 1–1½ lb main protein (chicken thigh, pork, beef flank, or shrimp as fits the dish)
  • ½ tsp baking soda + 1 Tbsp water (velveting, for stir-fry meats)
  • 1 Tbsp soy sauce + 1 tsp cornstarch + 1 tsp oil (marinade)
  • 2–3 cups neutral oil for frying (when crispy)
  • Sauce: 3 Tbsp soy sauce, 2 Tbsp sugar, 1 Tbsp rice vinegar, 1 Tbsp ketchup or oyster sauce as fits
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced; 1 tsp minced ginger
  • 1 cup mixed veg (broccoli, pepper, onion, or scallion)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil to finish
  • Steamed rice or noodles, for serving

How you do it

  1. Prep protein: cut bite-size. For stir-fries, toss with baking soda water 10 minutes, rinse, pat dry; then marinade 15 minutes.
  2. If the dish is crispy/buffet-style: dust with cornstarch (or dip batter), fry in 350°F oil until deep golden; drain on a rack.
  3. Build sauce in a bowl — taste it. Western-Chinese food lives or dies on that sweet-salty-tang balance.
  4. In a hot wok with a little oil, bloom garlic/ginger 10 seconds. Add veg if using; toss crisp-tender.
  5. Return protein; pour sauce. Toss until glossy and thick. Finish sesame oil. Serve immediately over rice or noodles.

Secrets of the Kitchen

  • Day-old cold rice only for fried rice — fresh rice turns to mush.
  • Oil hot enough: if the food pales and greases, your oil is too cool.
  • Sugar is not the enemy here — American Chinese lacquer needs it. Balance with vinegar and soy.
  • Cornstarch slurry at the end gives that restaurant sheen; add off the roaring boil so it doesn’t go gluey.
  • Taste the sauce in a spoon before it hits the wok. Adjust sweet/salt/acid like a bartender.

Kitchen note: Crispy pork cubes in the same bright sauce.

Crispy pork cubes in the same bright sauce. This version chases pure taste — crunchy, savory, a little sweet — no organs, no bitter oddities, no strange textures. Just what people actually order twice.

Serves 4

Ingredients

  • 1–1½ lb main protein (chicken thigh, pork, beef flank, or shrimp as fits the dish)
  • ½ tsp baking soda + 1 Tbsp water (velveting, for stir-fry meats)
  • 1 Tbsp soy sauce + 1 tsp cornstarch + 1 tsp oil (marinade)
  • 2–3 cups neutral oil for frying (when crispy)
  • Sauce: 3 Tbsp soy sauce, 2 Tbsp sugar, 1 Tbsp rice vinegar, 1 Tbsp ketchup or oyster sauce as fits
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced; 1 tsp minced ginger
  • 1 cup mixed veg (broccoli, pepper, onion, or scallion)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil to finish
  • Steamed rice or noodles, for serving

How you do it

  1. Prep protein: cut bite-size. For stir-fries, toss with baking soda water 10 minutes, rinse, pat dry; then marinade 15 minutes.
  2. If the dish is crispy/buffet-style: dust with cornstarch (or dip batter), fry in 350°F oil until deep golden; drain on a rack.
  3. Build sauce in a bowl — taste it. Western-Chinese food lives or dies on that sweet-salty-tang balance.
  4. In a hot wok with a little oil, bloom garlic/ginger 10 seconds. Add veg if using; toss crisp-tender.
  5. Return protein; pour sauce. Toss until glossy and thick. Finish sesame oil. Serve immediately over rice or noodles.

Secrets of the Kitchen

  • Day-old cold rice only for fried rice — fresh rice turns to mush.
  • Oil hot enough: if the food pales and greases, your oil is too cool.
  • Sugar is not the enemy here — American Chinese lacquer needs it. Balance with vinegar and soy.
  • Cornstarch slurry at the end gives that restaurant sheen; add off the roaring boil so it doesn’t go gluey.
  • Taste the sauce in a spoon before it hits the wok. Adjust sweet/salt/acid like a bartender.

  • ½ tsp white pepper
  • 1 Tbsp cornstarch + 2 Tbsp water
