Part 0 — Equipment & Pantry

Before the steam-table glory and the weeknight wok, stock the tools and bottles that make Chinese cooking taste like itself.

Taste first. A hot wok, a sharp cleaver, and five pantry bottles will get you further than a drawer full of gadgets. Build heat, build sauce, then eat while it’s still loud.

Equipment

Carbon-Steel Wok

High heat, fast toss, that whisper of wok hei. Season it once, keep it dry, and it cooks like a cast-iron cousin with better walls.

Cleaver

Chop, smash, scoop. One blade for garlic paste, vegetable ribbons, and moving everything from board to wok.

Bamboo Steamer

Gentle steam for bao, dumplings, and fish. Lined with cabbage or parchment so nothing sticks and nothing weeps.

Spider & Ladle

Fish fried bits from oil, lift noodles from water, finish sauces without losing the heat. The wok’s best friends.

Rice Cooker

Perfect rice on autopilot so the wok can stay loud. Day-old fridge rice is still the fried-rice secret.

Pantry

Light & Dark Soy

Light for salt and brightness; dark for color and molasses depth. Keep both — they are not twins.

Oyster Sauce

Umami lacquer for greens, beef, and stir-fries. A spoon turns plain into takeout-night shiny.

Toasted Sesame Oil

Finish, don’t fry. A few drops at the end wake noodles, dumplings, and cold salads.

Cornstarch

Velvet meat, glossy sauces, light dredges. Mix with cold water before it hits the wok.

Shaoxing Wine

The kitchen’s red wine: marinades, deglazes, and that round aroma Chinese kitchens lean on.

Ginger · Garlic · Scallion

The wok trinity. Bloom them in hot oil and the whole kitchen announces dinner.

General Tso's Chicken

Kitchen note: Crispy dark-meat chicken in a glossy sweet-chile lacquer — buffet throne food.

Crispy dark-meat chicken in a glossy sweet-chile lacquer — buffet throne food. This version chases pure taste — crunchy, savory, a little sweet — no organs, no bitter oddities, no strange textures. Just what people actually order twice.

Serves 4

Ingredients

  • 1–1½ lb main protein (chicken thigh, pork, beef flank, or shrimp as fits the dish)
  • ½ tsp baking soda + 1 Tbsp water (velveting, for stir-fry meats)
  • 1 Tbsp soy sauce + 1 tsp cornstarch + 1 tsp oil (marinade)
  • 2–3 cups neutral oil for frying (when crispy)
  • Sauce: 3 Tbsp soy sauce, 2 Tbsp sugar, 1 Tbsp rice vinegar, 1 Tbsp ketchup or oyster sauce as fits
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced; 1 tsp minced ginger
  • 1 cup mixed veg (broccoli, pepper, onion, or scallion)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil to finish
  • Steamed rice or noodles, for serving

How you do it

  1. Prep protein: cut bite-size. For stir-fries, toss with baking soda water 10 minutes, rinse, pat dry; then marinade 15 minutes.
  2. If the dish is crispy/buffet-style: dust with cornstarch (or dip batter), fry in 350°F oil until deep golden; drain on a rack.
  3. Build sauce in a bowl — taste it. Western-Chinese food lives or dies on that sweet-salty-tang balance.
  4. In a hot wok with a little oil, bloom garlic/ginger 10 seconds. Add veg if using; toss crisp-tender.
  5. Return protein; pour sauce. Toss until glossy and thick. Finish sesame oil. Serve immediately over rice or noodles.

Secrets of the Kitchen

  • Day-old cold rice only for fried rice — fresh rice turns to mush.
  • Oil hot enough: if the food pales and greases, your oil is too cool.
  • Sugar is not the enemy here — American Chinese lacquer needs it. Balance with vinegar and soy.
  • Cornstarch slurry at the end gives that restaurant sheen; add off the roaring boil so it doesn’t go gluey.
  • Taste the sauce in a spoon before it hits the wok. Adjust sweet/salt/acid like a bartender.

Garlic Bok Choy

Serves 4

Ingredients

  • 1–1½ lb main protein (chicken thigh, pork, beef flank, or shrimp as fits the dish)
  • ½ tsp baking soda + 1 Tbsp water (velveting, for stir-fry meats)
  • 1 Tbsp soy sauce + 1 tsp cornstarch + 1 tsp oil (marinade)
  • 2–3 cups neutral oil for frying (when crispy)
  • Sauce: 3 Tbsp soy sauce, 2 Tbsp sugar, 1 Tbsp rice vinegar, 1 Tbsp ketchup or oyster sauce as fits
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced; 1 tsp minced ginger
  • 1 cup mixed veg (broccoli, pepper, onion, or scallion)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil to finish
  • Steamed rice or noodles, for serving

How you do it

  3. Build sauce in a bowl — taste it. Western-Chinese food lives or dies on that sweet-salty-tang balance.
  4. In a hot wok with a little oil, bloom garlic/ginger 10 seconds. Add veg if using; toss crisp-tender.

Secrets of the Kitchen

Tomato Egg Drop Soup

Kitchen note: Home soup — tomato sweetness, eggy ribbons.

Home soup — tomato sweetness, eggy ribbons. This version chases pure taste — crunchy, savory, a little sweet — no organs, no bitter oddities, no strange textures. Just what people actually order twice.

Serves 4

Ingredients

  • 6 cups chicken broth
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • 2 Tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 Tbsp rice vinegar (more for hot & sour)
  • ½ tsp white pepper
  • 1 Tbsp cornstarch + 2 Tbsp water
  • Scallions, sliced
  • Optional: soft tofu cubes, wood ear, bamboo, shredded chicken

How you do it

  1. Bring broth to a simmer; season soy, vinegar, white pepper.
  2. Stir in cornstarch slurry until lightly thick.
  3. Slowly drizzle beaten egg while stirring circles — ribbons form.
  4. Add tofu/veg if using; simmer 1 minute. Taste and adjust.
  5. Finish scallions; serve hot.

Secrets of the Kitchen

  • White pepper is the soul of egg drop and hot & sour — black pepper tastes different.
  • Cornstarch first, then egg — reverse order and you get scrambled soup.
  • For hot & sour, vinegar + white pepper should make you inhale happily, not cough.
